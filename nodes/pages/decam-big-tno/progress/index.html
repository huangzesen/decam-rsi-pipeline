How Run 1 works

Run 1 has no hidden set. Each agent is given all 23 regions we have prepared, with every known body's detections, name and size, the full record of the earlier run, and every earlier program of this run with its results. It is asked for a general method that finds as many known large bodies as possible with as little GPU time as possible, and that also reports unknown ones. Four regions are graded: REG_A, REG_B, R009 and R013, with 113 known large bodies that have detections on at least 5 nights. A body counts as found when one candidate holds it on at least 5 nights, at least 80% of that candidate's detections are the body, and the candidate is in the top 226 by the program's own confidence (twice the number of known bodies, so there is room for as many new ones). Because the agent can see the answers, every program is read by hand for shortcuts: body names, stored positions, row numbers, settings per region. A candidate in the scored list that matches no known body is a lead and goes to the same checks as the earlier run's.

The earlier run (12–14 November 2026) used the first rules: three sealed regions the agent never saw, one score, an hour per region on one A100. It ended after round 36 with a best score of 0.6061. Its round 19 program is the starting program of Run 1. The Candidates and Data tabs describe this earlier run.

What the programs read

Each sealed region is one patch of sky near the ecliptic, about 54 deg², with every public DECam exposure that covers it over eleven years. The programs never see pixels. They read our own list of every source in every exposure: the Community Pipeline's calibrated images, searched on TACC with a filter matched to each CCD's seeing, keeping every peak at S/N 4.5 or more. Stars, galaxies, noise peaks, cosmic rays and asteroids are all in it; a distant body is a few dozen rows among tens of millions, moving a few arcseconds an hour.

Agents develop on a fourth, unscored region (about 35 million detections, 356 exposures) with no answers; the three regions above, and which known bodies are in them, stay sealed on our side and are used only to grade.

How the score and the checks work

Each sealed region holds every detection from single DECam exposures (S/N ≥ 4.5) across 2013–2026. The program reports candidates, each a set of detections with a confidence. All regions' candidates are ranked together; the scored list holds twice as many slots as there are known bodies with H ≤ 8 seen on at least 5 nights (62, so 124 slots). A known body counts as found when 80% of a candidate's detections are its own and cover 5 of its nights. Rounds 0 and 1 were first graded on Vista GB200 nodes and are regraded on NERSC A100s so every score compares on the same hardware. A lead goes to the novelty check against the MPC's files, then to an image check (is it detected where its orbit says it should be, and not at shifted places), then to images the program never saw; candidates that pass go to OBSERVER_NAME for follow-up, ten at a time.
