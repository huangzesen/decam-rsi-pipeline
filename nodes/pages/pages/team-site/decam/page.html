Whole-sky scan

Searching every public DECam exposure for large distant bodies

Thirteen years of the Dark Energy Camera's public archive, searched 5 % of its sky at a time by a program that AI agents wrote and keep improving. Each cell below is 3° × 3°; coloured cells have been searched, shaded ones are waiting.

5 % at a time

The slices

Each slice is about 1,580 deg² of DECam sky, chosen for repeated seasons of coverage near the ecliptic (later ones weighted to what the LDT can observe this winter). A slice is downloaded and run through source detection at NERSC, then searched region by region on NERSC A100 and Vista GH200 GPUs. Recall is the share of known bodies that the data could show which the program found.

The program

Written by agents, round after round

The search program comes from a hill climb: each round an agent reads every earlier program and its results, writes a new one, and it is graded on four sky regions by how many findable known bodies it recovers and how much GPU time it takes. Every 5 % slice runs the best program at the time it starts.
